I went to an experimental elementary school, so I never took the kind of segment exams common in junior high, and I wasn’t used to reviewing class material in a structured, exam-focused way. After I started 7th grade, preparing for those exams was a completely new experience—I adjusted my approach as I went. From 7th grade through the second semester of 8th grade, I went through about eleven segment exams, and my study workflow kept evolving.

## Method 1 — 7th grade, fall: first two exam rounds

- Before exams: Mind maps in Google Slides, plus Quizlet for practice questions and memorization.
- Night before: Read notes for the next day’s subjects, review the question bank, prep Chinese memorization.
- Exam day: Skim textbook highlights again; draw any mind maps I hadn’t finished.

Slides wasn’t a good long-term tool for mind maps, so I moved my notes to Google Sites.

## Method 2 — 7th grade, fall: third exam round

- Before exams: Google Sites for subject notes; Quizlet for banks and memorization.
- Night before: Same rhythm—next day’s notes, question bank, Chinese memorization.
- Exam day: Textbook highlights again.

Sites still didn’t work well as a real notebook, so I switched to Obsidian.

## Method 3 — 7th grade, spring: first two exam rounds

- Before exams: Obsidian for notes; Quizlet for banks and memorization.
- Night before: Notes for the next day, question bank, Chinese memorization.
- Exam day: Textbook highlights again.

History got heavier that semester. I added timelines to understand events better, and tried fill-in-the-blank sheets plus handwritten Chinese memorization drills to review more actively before exams.

## Method 4 — 7th grade, spring: third exam round

- At school: After finishing major-subject practice tests, and during a few self-study blocks in arts/PE-type classes, I prepared material that didn’t fit on a screen (e.g. history timelines), fill-in sheets I could use on exam days, and Chinese memorization.
- At home: Obsidian for notes; Quizlet for banks and memorization.
- Night before: Notes, question bank, Chinese memorization.
- Exam day: (Chinese: memorization first) complete the fill-ins, correct them, mark weak spots, then textbook highlights again.

Fill-in drills didn’t help me much, so I dropped that step.

## Method 5 — 8th grade, fall: first two exam rounds

- At school: Same idea—time after big practice tests and in select self-study periods—for timelines and Chinese memorization.
- At home: Obsidian for notes; Quizlet for memorization.
- Night before: Notes, question bank, Chinese memorization.
- Exam day: (Chinese: memorization first) textbook highlights again.

This version was the most effective segment-exam workflow for me. Obsidian was hard to print, which made paper review at school awkward, so I moved notes and memorization into Google Docs and printed them.

## Method 6 — 8th grade, fall: third exam round

- At school: Timelines during the same kinds of windows as above.
- At home: Google Docs for notes and memorization, printed; Quizlet for memorization.
- Night before: Notes, question bank, Chinese memorization.
- Exam day: (Chinese: memorization first) one pass through printed notes, then textbook highlights.

In the spring of 8th grade, history felt lighter, so I used Google Docs for everything.

## Method 7 — 8th grade, spring

- Before exams: Google Docs for notes and memorization (Chinese, English, geography, history, science—including Chinese/English memorization), printed; Quizlet for memorization.
- Night before: Notes, question bank, Chinese memorization.
- Exam day: (Chinese: memorization first) printed notes once, then textbook highlights.

---

Overall, Method 5 is the core routine I’d keep; adding Google Calendar and Google Tasks for scheduling makes it easier to finish everything before each exam round.
