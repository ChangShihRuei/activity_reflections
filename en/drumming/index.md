I started learning drum set in second grade of elementary school, and it has been almost eight years since then. From a beginner to a drummer now, I have learned a lot, and it has made me love drumming even more.

It began when I was listening to an orchestra and noticed a cool instrument called tubular bells. After asking my parents’ friends, I learned that the foundation of percussion usually starts with the drum set, so I officially began drum lessons. After studying for a while, I found that I was actually quite interested in drums, so I kept going—from beginner to more advanced levels, all the way through seventh grade. I also moved from the original studio to a place closer to home, which made commuting much more convenient.

In elementary school, I also joined the club “Rock Classroom,” where I learned how to work with other people in a band instead of just drumming on my own and doing whatever felt fun. I also started paying attention to whether I was blending well with other instruments, and whether the volume felt comfortable.

Later, my first teacher decided to study in Japan because of future plans, so I met my second teacher. From him I learned many different advanced techniques.

Most recently, my second teacher also paused teaching because of future plans, so I met my current teacher. Besides learning different techniques, I have also started studying improvisation, which gives me more room to grow and helps me learn how to create more freely.
